November 12, 2026

Muse can now call US businesses for you, if you pay

After a limited test that started in September, Muse can now place phone calls to US businesses on your behalf. Ask it to book a table, make an appointment, or sit on hold, and it handles the call and reports back. For now it is only for paid subscribers in the US. Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang announced it on November 12, pitching it for the calls nobody wants to make: insurance, cable, the DMV, dinner reservations. Plans start at $20 a month.

Source: TechCrunch — Muse can now place calls to U.S. businesses — if you pay, November 12, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: If you pay for Muse, you can hand off hold music and phone trees, but some businesses hang up when they spot an AI caller, so check the result before you count the job done. Free users do not get calling yet.

November 12, 2026

Muse gets its own email address

Muse Mail gives your Muse its own @muse.ai address. Forward it a bill or a booking, or add it to an email thread, and Muse can act on it for you — read it, reply to it, and keep track of it. The address belongs to your Muse, not you, and it is distinct from the Gmail and Outlook connectors, which link Muse to accounts you already own. Meta announced it at Connect on September 23, and it has been available to paid subscribers in the US and Canada since November 10, rolling out in waves — if the Setup card is not in your account yet, it has not reached you. The handle is yours to choose, and it is permanent. You set it up in the Muse app: tap the 'Give your Muse its own email' card, tap Setup, pick a handle, verify your own email address in settings, then tell Muse, which checks that the mailbox is live. Update (Nov 13): Wang said this week the Muse email address will soon roll out to free users too.

Source: All Things Muse — How to set up your Muse email, November 7, 2026 · TechCrunch — Muse can now place calls to U.S. businesses — if you pay, November 12, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: Email follow-ups, bills and bookings can now run through Muse without keeping the app open, but check the handle before you pick it — it cannot be changed later.

November 12, 2026

Two new connectors go live: Expedia hotels and Uber Courier

Muse can now book Expedia hotels in the US in plain language, and arrange an Uber Courier package delivery, all inside Muse chat. Expedia is live on Muse, and you can book a hotel stay directly in Muse chat, no Expedia app needed, and no separate booking site either. You pick the dates and the place, and Muse shows hotel options with total prices, and you keep control — ask Muse to show more options or ask for price, location, or any details first before you book, and it lets you see the final total before you confirm. Uber Courier: you can ask Muse to book a courier to deliver a package to someone else or pick one up. Uber said its in-chat courier booking is live now. For deliveries, you check the price in chat and confirm the booking before Uber picks the package up.

Source: Skift — Expedia goes live on Muse, November 9, 2026 · Uber on X — Uber Courier in Muse chat, November 10, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: Booking a hotel and sending a package are now jobs you can hand to Muse in one chat instead of opening another app, but check the booking details and the price yourself before you approve.

November 12, 2026

Nonprofits sue Meta and other AI labs to freeze frontier AI development

Four nonprofits sued Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and SpaceX's AI unit in a California court on November 12, asking a judge to freeze development and deployment of any AI system more capable than the labs' most capable systems as of that date. The suit, filed in San Francisco Superior Court under California's unfair competition law, argues the companies are building systems they know are unsafe. No court has ruled yet, and Meta declined to comment. This is a lawsuit, not a law, and it is separate from the superintelligence ban bills tracked in the Ban Tracker, which are still at Introduced with no hearing or vote.

Source: Reuters — OpenAI, Google, Anthropic hit with lawsuit seeking halt to frontier AI development, November 12, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: Nothing changes in your Muse account today, but if the court grants the freeze it could slow future Muse model upgrades, so watch this case alongside the Ban Tracker.

November 12, 2026

Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast model for everyday agent work

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12, the fast, everyday member of its Gemini 4 family, built for coding and multi-step agent work. It has a 1-million-token context window and API pricing of $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. It is rolling out across Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, the Gemini app, Google Search, and Gemini for business, with paid Pro and Ultra subscribers getting it in Google Antigravity.

Source: Google — Introducing Gemini 4 Hydrogen, November 12, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: Gemini is the model Muse is most often compared with; a faster, cheaper rival raises the bar for what Muse has to do for the same money, and it is another option if you run agent work outside Muse.

November 11, 2026

Muse reaches your wrist: Apple Watch and Wear OS apps

Meta released Muse for iOS 15.0 on November 9, adding a Muse companion app for Apple Watch and Wear OS watches. Talk to Muse by voice, send text, or dictate, from your wrist. The Apple Watch app needs watchOS 9.0 or later, and the iPhone app needs iOS 18.0 or newer.

November 11, 2026

Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, faster and cheaper

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9, the same underlying model under two safeguard levels. Fable is open to everyone; Mythos goes only to organizations that pass Anthropic's verification programs. The price drops from $10 and $50 to $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens, output runs up to 30 percent faster, and the context window is 1 million tokens.

Source: Anthropic

How this affects a Muse user: The rival top-end model just got cheaper and faster, which sets the bar Muse's own models are judged against. If you compare agents for heavy coding work, re-check prices before you commit to a paid plan. Nothing changes inside Muse itself.

November 11, 2026

Google adds two TPU systems for secure and long-context AI work

Google announced TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max on November 9, its third and fourth eighth-generation TPU systems in a single year. TPU 8tx is built for confidential inference and private fine-tuning; TPU 8i Max pairs two TPU 8i chips in one package for double the compute, memory, and bandwidth for massive context windows. Google published no prices and no availability date; reporting says 2027.

Source: Google Blog

How this affects a Muse user: The chips that run AI models are being built for agents specifically, with longer context and private runs. No change to Muse today, but it is a sign the cost of running long agent jobs should keep falling.

November 10, 2026

Muse Ember, the smallest and cheapest Muse model

Meta announced November 9 that Muse Ember is live now in Muse Code and on the Meta Model API as muse-ember-1.0. It is built for the repetitive work agents do all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction, and long document parsing. It has a 1M-token context window and up to 512K output tokens, and is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.004 per million. Meta says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best benchmark score at about a third of the cost per task, and it is the first model trained using Muse Wonder Full.

Source: Meta — Muse Ember, November 9, 2026

How this affects a Muse user: If you run agents that repeat the same small jobs hundreds of times a session, Ember is the cheap engine for that work, while Wonder and Spark handle harder coding; everyday use of the Muse app itself does not change, Ember is an API and Muse Code model.

Field discoveries

  • Thumbnail-first cover design: most buyers meet your book as a 200-pixel thumbnail in search results — test your cover at thumbnail size before publishing.
  • Test demand before you build: sales-rank data on existing titles tells you whether a niche has buyers — validate first, build second.
  • Render it, then read the actual page: a generated file can report the right page count while quietly truncating text — export the real file, open it, and read it, especially instruction pages.
  • Stop at a confirmed block: if a platform confirms you are blocked, stop. No bypass, no retry loop, no scraper.
  • Low competition can mean low demand — and tokens aren't cash: price both in dollars and weeks before spending time on either. Count invite code GZTOI6 as working token credit in Settings > Redeem token, never as income in a hustle plan.
