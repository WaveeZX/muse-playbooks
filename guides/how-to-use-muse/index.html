How to use Muse AI

Give Muse a real job, check the result, and use it again tomorrow. Start free, upgrade only when you hit the limit most weeks.

Muse is a personal AI agent that can do real work for you, not just answer questions. This guide shows you how to use Muse AI in 10 minutes: what to ask, how to give it a job, how to check its work, and how to turn one good result into a repeatable playbook.

What Muse is, in plain words

Muse connects to your email, calendar, files, and apps you allow, then does tasks for you, like drafting a reply, building a list, checking a price, or preparing a brief. You give it a job, a finish line, and a limit. It does the work, you check it against the original source, then you use it or fix it.

How to use Muse AI, 5 steps

  1. Pick one real job. Not a test. A job with a finish line you can see, like 'draft a reply to this email' or 'make a list of what is due tomorrow'.
  2. Give Muse the job, the finish line, and the limit. Say what good looks like, what it must not do, and when to stop and ask you.
  3. Give it only the context it needs. One email, one file, one list. Extra context makes extra mistakes.
  4. Run it, then check the result against the original source. Check names, dates, amounts, and links yourself before you use it.
  5. Save what worked as a prompt you can reuse. If it worked once, it is a playbook. If it missed, say what was missing and run it once more.

Prompt to copy

Do this job for me: [describe the job]. It is done when [say exactly what good looks like]. Use only [name the source]. Do not send, buy, book, delete, or change anything without my approval. Show me what you used and what is still uncertain.

First prompts to try, Muse AI prompts tutorial

  • "Make a one-page brief for tomorrow from my calendar and the emails that need a reply. Do not send or reply to anything."
  • "Turn this long email thread into 5 bullet points: what was decided, what is still open, and who owes what."
  • "Draft a short reply to this message in my voice. Keep it plain and short. Do not send it."
  • "Build a simple tracker for [goal, target number, deadline]. Give me a table with date, number, and one line for what I did that week."
